Data, attribution & accuracy

Real geometry, generated game details

Road centerlines and simple closed building outlines are sourced from OpenStreetMap. Mapped height or building-level tags are used where present. Missing heights are estimated; very tall buildings and façade detail are simplified for performance. Complex multipolygon buildings and courtyards are not reconstructed in this prototype. Road widths default to estimates when missing.

Terrain is sampled from Mapzen Terrain Tiles using the Terrarium elevation encoding, interpolated onto a game mesh and normalized to the selected center. Pixel spacing is not source accuracy. Sources vary; much global data is tens-of-metres resolution and may be dated. Terrain can capture neighborhood slopes but cannot reliably identify individual foundations, doors, retaining walls or split-level floors. Door sills are placed at sampled ground at the opening and are never below that ground. Interior floors follow the sill; downhill foundations are approximate. When a mapped fence rings a house and the DEM shows about a half-storey drop, the yard between fence and house is raised to house level as an educated guess. Missing elevation produces an explicitly labeled flat fallback.

Parks, forest and water patches use OpenStreetMap landuse, leisure and natural ways when those rings are closed. They tint or overlay the draped ground. Mapped barrier ways (fence, wall, hedge) and tree nodes are placed as game objects; extra trees are scattered on forest and park patches. They are not surveyed plantings. All windows, doors, roof fixtures, façade materials and enemy placements are generated for gameplay. Generated character and weapon art is illustrative. This is a fictional game using geographic data, not a survey, navigation product or representation of actual occupants.

Satellite minimap

EOxCloudless by EOX IT Services GmbH (Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2020–2025). Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0. Imagery is included for this private, non-commercial game. The imagery remains under that license; commercial use requires an appropriate separate license. The loader asks EOX for the newest public cloudless mosaic first (2025, then 2024, 2023, 2020) at native zoom 14, which is the finest tile matrix EOX publishes and matches Sentinel-2 RGB (~10 metres per pixel). The same tiles are draped on the 3D terrain mesh and shown on the minimap with a player marker. A repeating procedural grain is multiplied on the ground so walking-scale pixels are not a flat smear; that grain is not photographic. Native imagery is still about 10 metres per pixel, so it is less detailed than high-resolution aerial photography. Imagery dates and OSM mapping dates may differ.

Bundled fallback tiles (2020 mosaic, used only if live EOX is unreachable): Selected area, Tel Aviv, Barcelona west, Barcelona east. Other locations request tiles from EOX, subject to coverage and service availability. No Google Maps or Street View imagery is collected, analyzed, or included.

You can also build a stage from a north-up aerial photograph you provide. The photo is draped on the ground and used to guess roofs, tree canopies and grassy yards. That file stays in your browser; it is not uploaded to a third-party map service. If coordinates are filled in, OpenStreetMap outlines and elevation are kept and the photo replaces Sentinel-2. Photo-derived geometry is an educated guess, not a survey. Do not use imagery you are not allowed to copy. Official GovMap / Survey of Israel tiles are not fetched by the game.

Prototype limits

Random location selects among three bundled, checked geographic areas. Other coordinates are loaded live and depend on external service availability and mapping coverage. Doors are generated openings you can walk through; some are low enough to need a crouch. Surveyed façades, automatic super-resolution of satellite pixels, and exact floor identification are not implemented. Road markings and surface materials are generated. Sentinel-2 is draped at its native ~10 m/pixel; close-up grain is procedural. Both camera views use the same world. Aim assist can be switched off for manual horizontal and vertical aiming. WASD moves relative to your horizontal aiming direction; looking up does not make the character fly. Space jumps; small upward steps are taken automatically.
